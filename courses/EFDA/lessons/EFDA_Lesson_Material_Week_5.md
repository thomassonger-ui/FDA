## EFDA107 · Alginate Impressions

### What an alginate impression is for

An alginate impression captures the shape and detail of teeth and surrounding soft tissue in a hydrocolloid material that sets quickly inside the mouth. The set impression is removed, rinsed, and either poured immediately with dental stone or sent to a lab. The poured model is used for:

- Study models — record of patient's current dentition
- Diagnostic models — orthodontic planning, occlusal analysis
- Provisional crown matrices (Week 3)
- Bleaching tray fabrication
- Custom impression tray fabrication
- Mouthguard fabrication

Alginate is an "irreversible hydrocolloid" — once it sets, you can't re-flow it. Compare to "reversible hydrocolloid" (agar), which is heat-reversible but rarely used today.

### Properties to understand

- **Mixing time** — typically 30 seconds (manufacturer-specific). Too short = lumpy mix. Too long = early set.
- **Working time** — total time from start of mix to seating the tray. Typically 60–90 seconds in the mouth.
- **Set time** — typically 90 seconds to 3 minutes in the mouth (warmer water = faster set).
- **Tear strength** — moderate. Avoid undercuts that "lock" the tray to the teeth.
- **Dimensional stability** — alginate dries out and distorts within hours. Pour the impression within 15 minutes or wrap in a damp paper towel in a sealed bag if delaying.

### Armamentarium

- **Stock impression trays** — perforated, in maxillary and mandibular sizes (small/medium/large for each arch)
- **Tray adhesive** (optional but recommended for perforated trays)
- **Alginate powder** + manufacturer's measuring scoop
- **Water** at room temperature (cooler water = slower set; warmer = faster)
- **Measuring cylinder or supplied dispenser**
- **Flexible mixing bowl** + stiff mixing spatula
- **Saliva ejector**, HVE
- **Bib for patient**, surface barriers
- **Disposable gloves** (changed after impression — alginate residue can transfer)

### Choosing and trying in the tray

Before mixing, try in the tray empty:
- Should extend 2–3 mm beyond the most distal tooth (and cover all dentition)
- Should clear the buccal and lingual frena
- Should clear the floor of the mouth (mandibular) and the soft palate posteriorly (maxillary)
- If you have a poor fit, switch to another size or beading wax for additions

### Mixing technique

1. Measure alginate powder (1 scoop per arch typically; check brand instructions)
2. Measure water to match (room-temp water for normal set; cooler for slower set)
3. Pour water in the bowl first, then powder on top — reduces dust inhalation
4. Mix by pressing alginate against the side of the bowl with the flat of the spatula, rotating the bowl
5. Mix vigorously for the manufacturer's recommended time (typically 30 seconds)
6. Result should be smooth, creamy, with no powder lumps or excessive air

### Loading and seating

1. Load the tray with one bulk fill from one end to the other (avoid layering, which creates voids)
2. Smooth the upper surface; wet a finger and press the alginate into the deepest curves
3. For maxillary: insert from one side, rotate over the dentition, center, and push up firmly. Push posteriorly first, anteriorly second.
4. For mandibular: ask patient to lift tongue. Center the tray, push down firmly. Ask patient to relax the tongue.
5. Hold the tray firmly with steady pressure until set
6. Test for set: press a finger lightly on the alginate outside the tray; should be firm and not stick

### Removing and inspecting

1. Break the air seal at one edge with a finger
2. Snap the tray straight off the dentition (don't rock it — distorts the impression)
3. Inspect for: complete coverage, sharp detail of cusps and embrasures, no voids in critical areas, no tear in the alginate around the teeth
4. If poor — retake. Diagnostic-quality is the standard.
5. Rinse gently under cool water to remove saliva
6. Disinfect per office protocol (typically a hospital disinfectant spray, 10 minutes)

### Pouring the model

1. Mix dental stone with water to manufacturer ratio
2. Vibrate the impression to remove air bubbles
3. Flow stone into one corner of the impression; let it run down to fill the deepest parts
4. Continue adding stone until the impression is full
5. Add a base of stone if the model is freestanding
6. Allow to set 30–60 minutes
7. Carefully separate the model from the alginate
8. Trim the model on a model trimmer for clean borders

### Common errors

- **Distorted impression** — tray rocked during removal; or alginate stretched on the way out → retake
- **Bubbles in critical areas** — alginate not pressed into deep curves; tray loaded with layered fills
- **Tear in alginate** — undercuts not blocked, or alginate too thin in some areas → switch to thicker mix or block undercuts with wax
- **Set too fast in summer / too slow in winter** — water temp not adjusted for season → use cooler water in summer, warmer in winter

## EFDA110 · Periodontal Dressing

### What a periodontal dressing does

After periodontal surgery (gingivectomy, flap surgery, gingival graft), the surgeon often applies a periodontal dressing — a putty-like protective covering over the surgical site. It serves four purposes:

- Mechanical protection from food impaction during initial healing
- Holds the flap or graft in position
- Reduces patient discomfort
- Reduces bleeding through mild pressure

Dressings stay in place 7–10 days, after which they're removed (and sometimes replaced if healing isn't complete).

### Two main types

**Zinc oxide eugenol (ZOE) — Coe-Pak (most common)**

Two-paste system: base (zinc oxide + oils) + accelerator (eugenol + rosin). Mixed in equal lengths to a workable putty. Sets in the presence of saliva over 2–3 minutes.

**Non-eugenol (Periocare, Barricaid)**

Photopolymerizing or chemical-cure versions. Used when eugenol is contraindicated (rare allergic reactions, certain orthodontic adhesives nearby).

### Placement (assisting the surgeon)

1. Surgeon completes the surgical procedure
2. Hemostasis verified — bleeding stopped
3. Mix the dressing per manufacturer (paper pad, stiff spatula)
4. Roll the mix into a thin cylinder
5. Place over the surgical site, adapting to the gingiva
6. Cover the buccal AND lingual surfaces of the involved teeth
7. Press against the gingiva (NOT into the interproximal where it could be dislodged)
8. Adapt the dressing to be smooth — sharp edges irritate cheek/tongue
9. Check occlusion — dressing should not interfere with bite
10. Patient instructions: don't eat hot/sharp/hard food on that side; rinse gently with warm saline tonight

### Removal (EFDA scope)

After 7–10 days, remove the dressing:

1. Insert a sturdy blunt instrument (surgical hoe or curet) under the edge of the dressing
2. Apply gentle lateral pressure — the dressing should loosen
3. Lift the dressing off in pieces
4. Use suction to remove debris
5. Rinse the surgical site with saline
6. Inspect the surgical site for healing status — note any concerning findings for the surgeon
7. Remove any remaining sutures (often combined with this visit)
8. Do NOT replace the dressing without surgeon authorization

### Common findings post-dressing

- Slight pink granulation tissue → normal healing
- Frank pus or significant erythema → notify surgeon
- Dressing came off early → notify surgeon, may need to replace
- Underlying flap displaced → notify surgeon immediately

## EFDA112 · Suture Removal

### Identifying sutures before removing

You need to know which sutures are in the field. Two categories:

| Type | Removal? |
|---|---|
| **Non-absorbable (silk, nylon, polypropylene)** | Removed by EFDA at 7–10 days |
| **Absorbable (gut, polyglactin, polydioxanone)** | Dissolve on their own; usually no removal needed |

If you can't tell which type it is, look in the patient's chart — the surgeon documents suture material. When in doubt, ask.

### Suture patterns

- **Interrupted** — each stitch is independent; you can remove some and leave others if healing is uneven
- **Continuous (running)** — one long thread loops between bites; removing one bite affects the rest. Cut in multiple places to remove cleanly.
- **Figure-eight** — looped pattern crossing the wound; cut at the cross
- **Sling** — wrapped around a tooth; clip at the gingival side, slide free

### Timing

- **3–5 days** — early removal for superficial intraoral wounds healing fast
- **7–10 days** — standard for oral surgical sites (extractions, flaps)
- **10–14 days** — extraoral skin sutures or delayed healing

Sutures left too long become an irritant and a chronic infection nidus. Remove on schedule.

### Armamentarium

- Suture removal scissors (sharp, fine tip — NOT cotton scissors which crush)
- College pliers or hemostat
- Cotton gauze
- Saline or chlorhexidine rinse
- Good light + suction
- Sometimes topical anesthetic for sensitive patients

### Technique

1. Identify the suture knot
2. Clean the suture with saline-soaked gauze (removes biofilm and food debris)
3. Grasp the knot gently with college pliers
4. Slip one blade of the scissors under the suture, between the knot and the gingiva
5. Cut as close to the gingiva as possible (so the longer end is pulled through tissue, not the contaminated knot)
6. Pull the suture out along the long axis of the wound (not perpendicular)
7. Note suture count in vs. count out — confirm complete removal
8. Inspect healing; document
9. Rinse the area with saline
10. Patient instructions per surgeon

### Pitfalls

- **Cutting close to the knot side** — pulls a contaminated portion through clean tissue. Always cut near the tissue surface, not the knot.
- **Forceful pulling** — can disrupt fragile granulation tissue. Loosen knot first; pull gently.
- **Missed suture** — count in and count out. A retained suture causes inflammation.
- **Misidentified absorbable suture** — confirm in chart. Pulling an absorbable suture can disrupt the wound.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Alginate is irreversible hydrocolloid.** Once it sets, it's set. Mix correctly, seat once, remove cleanly.
2. **Match water temperature to working conditions.** Warmer = faster set; cooler = slower.
3. **Pour the impression within 15 minutes** or wrap in damp gauze. Alginate distorts as it dries.
4. **Periodontal dressing stays 7–10 days.** Remove gently with a blunt instrument; assess healing; do not replace without surgeon authorization.
5. **Identify sutures before removing.** Non-absorbable comes out; absorbable stays.
6. **Cut close to the gingiva, NOT close to the knot.** Pull the suture along the long axis of the wound.
7. **Count in, count out.** Retained suture = retained inflammation.
8. **All three procedures require sharp inspection.** Note healing findings and report concerns to the supervising dentist.
