# REFERENCE — Typodont limitation

Canonical explanation of why typodont X-rays don't count as radiographic proof in this course, what they ARE useful for, and what students should use instead. Effective 2026-05-16.

---

## The limitation

A **typodont** is the educational mannequin used in dental assisting and dental hygiene programs — a jaw model with plastic teeth set into a polymer base. Typodonts are essential for hands-on placement practice (positioning, instrument handling, mounts, intraoral landmark identification).

**Typodonts are not radio-opaque.** Their plastic teeth and polymer base do not absorb X-rays the way real enamel, dentin, and bone do. An X-ray exposure of a typodont produces a near-blank image — no readable anatomy, no structures to diagnose. Some commercial typodonts include limited radiopaque fillers in select teeth (e.g., for demonstration of restorations), but the model as a whole is not designed to produce diagnostic-quality radiographs.

**Consequence:** A typodont X-ray attempt does not demonstrate radiographic competency. It demonstrates only that a student knows how to push the exposure button. It does NOT demonstrate ALARA, technique, image evaluation, or any of the FAC 64B5-9.011 competencies the certification requires.

---

## When typodonts ARE useful in this course

Typodonts remain a core teaching tool for:

- Placement practice (XCP holder assembly, paralleling, bisecting angle)
- Bitewing positioning and beam alignment training
- Intraoral landmark identification
- Mounting practice (placing exposed images onto correct anatomical positions on a mount)
- Instrument handling without patient risk
- Repeatable, low-stakes drill before any patient contact

The Simulation Prep lessons in Week 6 (cmids 225, 226, 227) are explicitly typodont-friendly — they're about placement and beam angulation, not about producing diagnostic images.

---

## What students should submit instead for Capstone radiographic evidence

When the Capstone requires evidence of radiographic competency (cmid 238), accept these:

1. **Placement photos** showing correct positioning on a coworker (XCP visible, beam alignment, lead apron in place, etc.)
2. **Dentist verification** of the student's clinical technique — the Clinical Verification Form (cmid 206) signed by their supervising Florida-licensed dentist is the authoritative attestation
3. **Anonymized clinical images** from real chair-side work — must be patient-de-identified per HIPAA: no name, no DOB, no chart number, no jewelry/tattoos visible
4. **Original explanatory artifacts** (infographic / short video / patient-facing handbook) that demonstrate the student's radiographic knowledge in a teaching format — competency-by-explanation, valid alternative

## Where this is surfaced in the live course

- **Capstone Final Submission (cmid 238)** — neutral-gray "A note on typodonts" callout sitting between Acceptable artifact types and Artifact requirements. Highest-visibility placement, addresses the misconception at the moment of submission.
- **Capstone Rubric & Self-Check Guide (cmid 239)** — clarifier paragraph under the Content Accuracy criterion explaining typodont X-rays are not acceptable evidence.
- **Week 6 Overview (cmid 198)** — brief inline note near the Simulation Prep section explaining typodonts are for placement practice, real-patient evidence required for Capstone.

## For future course authors

When designing assessments that require "radiographic competency" proof, do not accept typodont X-rays. The default acceptable evidence types are listed above and reflected in `02-assessments/REFERENCE-capstone-submission-spec.md`. If a future assessment introduces a NEW evidence type, update this REFERENCE doc + the submission spec + any visible student-facing callouts together.
