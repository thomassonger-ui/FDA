# REFERENCE — Capstone Rubric

The 5-criterion analytic rubric used to grade the Capstone Final Submission (cmid 238). Same rubric across all three artifact format options (Infographic / Short Video / Patient Education Handout). Lives on the live course as cmid 239 (Capstone Rubric & Self-Check Guide).

---

## Grading rule

Each submission is rated on **5 criteria** at one of **3 performance levels**: Exemplary (3), Proficient (2), Developing (1).

**To earn Complete, every criterion must score Proficient (2) or higher.** Any single Developing rating returns the submission with feedback.

The capstone is graded on a **Complete / Needs Revision** basis — the analytic rubric exists for instructor feedback. Maximum grade = 50 points; pass = 38 (76%).

---

## The 5 criteria

### 1. Content Accuracy
| Exemplary (3) | Proficient (2) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|
| All radiography content is correct. No clinical or regulatory errors. Aligns precisely with Bird & Robinson and FAC 64B5-9.011 / §466.024(6) F.S. | Mostly accurate. One minor oversimplification or imprecise term that does not affect patient safety or compliance. | Multiple errors, or one significant error that could mislead a learner, patient, or co-worker. |

### 2. Synthesis & Depth of Understanding
| Exemplary (3) | Proficient (2) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|
| Connects concepts across the week. Draws inferences. Applies content to a novel scenario or audience. Demonstrates teaching insight, not just recall. | Accurate recall plus basic application. Concepts are explained correctly but stick close to the textbook's framing. | Restates content without analysis. Reads like notes copied from a source. No personal synthesis visible. |

### 3. Audience Fit
| Exemplary (3) | Proficient (2) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch is exactly right for the named audience (patient, new DA, colleague) throughout — vocabulary, examples, level of detail, tone all calibrated. | Mostly appropriate. One or two passages drift in vocabulary, jargon, or level of detail. | Audience misaligned. Too technical for a patient, or too simplistic for a colleague. Reading level or tone is wrong throughout. |

### 4. Craft (judged per chosen format)
| Exemplary (3) | Proficient (2) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|
| **Infographic:** Clear hierarchy, legible type, intentional color, no overcrowding. **Video:** Audible audio, steady framing, on-screen text supports voiceover, no dead air. **Handout:** Clean layout, scannable headings, key points pop. | Adequate craft for the chosen format. Structure is clear but layout, visuals, or audio could be sharper. One or two issues that don't prevent comprehension. | Craft distracts from the message. Typos, grainy video, muffled audio, illegible chart, or visual clutter that hurts the artifact's usability. |

### 5. Metacognition Statement & Sources
| Exemplary (3) | Proficient (2) | Developing (1) |
|---|---|---|
| 200+ words in your own voice. Clearly answers all three prompts (why this week, why this format, what you learned about your own learning). Bird & Robinson + 2+ course sources cited. Any AI use disclosed. | 200+ words present, partially answers the prompts, mostly in your voice. Bird & Robinson cited; one course source. AI disclosure present. | Under 200 words, vague, or generic. Misses prompts. Missing citations. AI use undisclosed where it appears used. |

## Self-check (student-facing, before submission)

- [ ] Every fact in my artifact is something I could defend if a Florida-licensed dentist asked "where does that come from?"
- [ ] I can explain at least one connection between concepts that isn't literally written in the textbook.
- [ ] If I read this aloud to my chosen audience, would they actually understand it? Where would they get lost?
- [ ] Layout / audio / typos — have I done a final pass?
- [ ] My metacognition statement is at least 200 words, in my voice, and answers all three prompts.
- [ ] Bird & Robinson is cited. At least one course lesson or Pressbook activity is cited. AI use (if any) is disclosed.

---

## Common reasons submissions are returned

1. **Wrong audience.** Patient handout that uses "collimation" without defining it; technical infographic that defines "X-ray" for a colleague.
2. **Recall, not synthesis.** Reads like a summary of the textbook chapter rather than a personal teaching artifact.
3. **Metacognition under 200 words**, generic, or written in third-person/AI voice.
4. **Missing citations** or undisclosed AI assistance.
5. **Clinical or regulatory error** that would mislead the audience or violate FAC 64B5-9.011 · FDOE Std 17 · §466.024(6) F.S.
