# REFERENCE — Self-Reflection Prompts (canonical set)

The actual prompts used on the 6 live Self-Reflection assignments (cmids 231–236). Each week has 3 prompts. **All prompts are week-specific — no reuse across weeks.** 200-word minimum across the three prompts.

---

## Week 1 — Foundations of Radiography (cmid 231)

1. **ALARA in your operatory.** What ALARA decision will look different in your practice tomorrow because of this week's content? Be specific — name the technique, the patient situation, or the workflow.
2. **Your toughest concept.** Which Week 1 topic was most challenging for you (radiation production, dose units, biological effects, ALARA, shielding)? What's your concrete plan to master it before clinical practice?
3. **Rectangular collimation in real practice.** Describe one situation in your current or future practice where rectangular collimation will materially reduce patient dose. If your office doesn't use rectangular collimators today, what would it take to advocate for the switch?

## Week 2 — Dental Imaging, Film & Processing (cmid 232)

1. **Receptor reality at your office.** Which receptor system does your office use today (CCD/CMOS sensor, PSP plate, F-speed film)? After this week, would you keep it or advocate for a change — and why?
2. **Your toughest concept.** Which Week 2 topic was hardest for you (digital sensor handling, processing chemistry sequence, mounting orientation, density vs. contrast)? What's your concrete plan to master it?
3. **Quality control in your workflow.** Pick one image-quality issue you'll watch for going forward (light film, dark film, fogged film, mounting flip, contact overlap). What will you check before the patient gets up from the chair?

## Week 3 — Compliance, Infection Control & QA (cmid 233)

1. **Infection-control gap.** Identify one infection-control practice in your operatory that you'll change or tighten because of this week (sensor barrier sequence, hand hygiene timing, surface disinfection, processing of contaminated images). Be specific.
2. **Compliance comprehension.** Which compliance concept was hardest for you (FAC 64B5-9.011 supervision rules, §466.024(6) F.S. statutory authority, scope-of-practice limits, retake documentation)? What's your concrete plan to lock it in?
3. **The patient question you're ready for.** Pick one common patient question ("Is this safe for my baby?", "Why so many X-rays?", "Aren't these dangerous?"). Draft, in your own words, the calm, accurate, scope-appropriate answer you'll give.

## Week 4 — Intraoral Imaging (cmid 234)

1. **The technique you'll drill.** Pick one specific intraoral skill you'll rehearse on a typodont before clinical practice (XCP assembly, posterior PA placement, BW horizontal alignment, anterior PA receptor seating). What does "mastered" look like for that skill?
2. **The error you're most worried about.** Of the common technique errors (cone cut, foreshortening, elongation, overlap, missed apices), which is most likely to trip you up first chairside? What's your specific plan to catch it before retake?
3. **Paralleling vs. bisecting.** Describe a patient situation where you'd switch from paralleling to bisecting technique (shallow palate, large tori, gag reflex, pediatric anatomy). Walk through your decision and what you'd document.

## Week 5 — Extraoral Imaging (cmid 235)

1. **Extraoral over intraoral.** Describe a specific clinical scenario where the dentist would order an extraoral image (panoramic, ceph, CBCT, Waters, Towne) instead of an intraoral series. Why is the extraoral the right call here?
2. **Your toughest extraoral concept.** Which Week 5 topic was hardest for you (panoramic focal trough, cephalometric standardization, CBCT vs medical CT, choosing among skull projections)? What's your concrete plan to master it?
3. **Patient positioning detail you'll watch for.** Pick one panoramic positioning fault (chin tilt up, chin tilt down, slumped posture, head twisted, tongue off palate, jewelry left in). What's your pre-exposure check to prevent it?

## Week 6 — Capstone Synthesis (cmid 236)

1. **One specific clinical change.** Identify one specific change you will make in your practice as a direct result of this 14-hour program. Be concrete — the technique, the workflow, the documentation, or the patient communication you'll do differently starting Monday.
2. **Your remaining gap.** Identify one area where you still feel less than confident after completing the program (a technique, a regulation, a piece of equipment, a patient scenario). What's your concrete plan to close that gap in the next 30 days?
3. **Demonstrating compliance.** How will you demonstrate ALARA practice and proper supervision compliance to your supervising Florida-licensed dentist? Walk through the evidence trail you'll create — documentation, retake logs, dosimeter wear, image quality reviews.
